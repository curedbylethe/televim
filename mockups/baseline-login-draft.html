Composing, in Insert mode

The bar is always a draft: it holds what the reader last typed, survives a conversation switch, and grows to six rows because a draft is their own words and the bar is the only place those words can be read back. Every space is a · while the draft is being composed — a space is a cell that paints nothing, and a caret on a blank cell is a bar on a blank cell, so the key that typed one looked like the key that did nothing. The dot stands in the space's own cell, which is what keeps the caret and the wrap in step. The conversation gets no dots: a message is read as prose.

TestBackend assertions this frame is the reference for

  • The bar's border is border-focused when the line has the focus, because the bar does not take the focus visually — it is the focus.
  • The title is Message to Ada Lovelace , not Message : a draft belongs to no conversation, and where it will be sent is the one thing a reader cannot work out for themselves.
  • Every space in the draft is · in text-dim; no message row has one.
  • The label is INSERT — the line's own mode, not the conversation's, because it is the only mode that is not the conversation's.
  • The caret is the terminal's, drawn at the row and column the wrapper worked out. TestBackend cannot model it: the arithmetic is tested, the cursor is checked by hand.
  • The hint is ⏎: send ^J: newline shift+⏎: newline where supported — 60 columns of the 71 available, because a hint that does not fit fails the build.
