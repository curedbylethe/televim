Profile panel, signed in — the reference frame

The panel is in the conversation's rectangle, not a third column: 30 / 70 is unchanged, the chat list is still there, and a profile is what the right-hand column holds. Focus is on it, so its border is the focused one — and that border is the only thing on screen saying where a keystroke goes. The rows are the ones the account has something to say about and nothing else: no bio, and no row for the bio.

TestBackend assertions this frame is the reference for

    • Exactly one of the three pane corners carries border-focused, and it is the profile at (24, 0) — the same corner the conversation lights, which is what saying “it took the conversation's slot” means. the_conversation_is_where_it_was is the other half, and a change that lit the border in both states would say nothing.
    • The title is Profile : the subject, not the command. :settings keeps its name.
    • The identity is one row — @ada · +15551234567 — because the identity is one thing and a reader scanning for “which account is this” wants one line to find it on. The · is the title-note joiner the status line already uses.
    • id, session, add account and logout are text-dim. The last two are the ones d acts on, and they are inert: a row that looks live and does nothing is worse than a row that says it is not.
    • A bio wider than the panel takes more rows and is never truncated — it is the reader's own words about themselves and the panel does not decide what they get to say.
    • The status row is the profile's hint, not the conversation's. dd:del and e:edit on a panel where neither applies is worse than silence: it tells the reader the keys are wrong rather than that they are not shown.
